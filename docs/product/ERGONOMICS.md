# Professional Ergonomics Spec v0.1

## Design target

The product is judged after hours of writing, not after a polished onboarding tour.

## Attention budget

Every persistent element must reduce one of:

- location uncertainty;
- save anxiety;
- retrieval time;
- structural effort;
- export uncertainty;
- recovery risk.

Anything else is summonable or removed.

## Motor budget

- Core drafting and navigation require no pointer.
- Repeated actions have stable shortcuts.
- Drag and drop always has keyboard equivalents.
- Small targets are not used for essential commands.
- Pane toggles and quick open avoid long pointer travel.

## Visual budget

- One dominant visual object per viewport: the prose surface.
- Stable line measure and scroll anchoring.
- No animated ambient analysis.
- Warning density is governed by severity and mode.
- The interface does not accumulate badges after long sessions.

## Interruption recovery

Measure time from app focus or task switch to meaningful continuation. Target routine resume under five seconds for recent work and under fifteen seconds after a moderate interruption.

## Fatigue testing

At hours 1, 3, and 6 record:

- hesitation before common actions;
- accidental mode changes;
- cursor or scroll loss;
- unprocessed warnings;
- search detours;
- save checking behavior;
- visual and physical discomfort.

## Failure criterion

A feature that saves time in a demo but increases uncertainty or residue across a six-hour session does not pass professional ergonomics.
